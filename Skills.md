Skills are your characters ability to do specific check, for example picking a lock could use a verity of skills the more board skill of "Thievery" or the very specific skill of "Lock picking" but not having a skill to use doesn't prevent you from making the check if you aren't proficient in a skill you just use the associated ability instead so for this example it would be you Dexterity ability. However skills can pull from different abilities, for example Lock picking is normally Dexterity representing the small and fine movements one must take. But lock picking could also be Intelligence representing your knowledge of the lock and its weaknesses

When notating your training in skills there's two ways you can take using the "Lock picking" example from earlier and a Dex score of +2 (this will come up later) the two formatting options are 
	Lock picking: +3 (dex) 
		This example notates the Training modifier of your skill and the tied skill so when rolling lock picking with this example you add a +5 to the roll (3 from skill, 2 dex). This has the down side of needing to do the addition for the end result but that is also its upside of if the Tied skill is changed in anyway it might be easier to notice that your lock picking also changed with it
	Lock picking: +5
		This examples notates the total modifier of your skill being the end result of what you add to the dice roll (3 from skill, 2 dex). This has the upside of being quicker and easier a majority of the time, but might make it likely to miss that when an ability changes that your total with lock picking also changed

Having a skill is much more useful then simply making the end result larger but changes the flavor of the success, for example being asked to roll a Persuasion check but not being trained in persuasion means you need to roll just a standard Charisma check, even if you succeeded the flavor and manor of that success is much more in the Hands of the GM the the intended Persuasion

# Skills at character creation
Before becoming you had a life of learning giving you a starting kit of skills for the start of your journey you gain a number of skill points which you can spend on skills, to determine the number of skill points you start with (3/7, 3/6, 9/7 or 14/12) 
## Cost
~~Gaining training in a skill with a +0 skill modifier: 1 skill point~~
Increasing skill modifier by +1 to a max of 3: 1 skill point
Increasing skill modifier by +1 past a skill modifier of +3: 2 skill points

# Gaining Skills
From Iron rank adventurers too even Amethyst Level adventurers through out your life, story and adventure you are likely to learn some new tricks, for example a standard town guard turn adventurer or even a decorated war hero might both through out their time adventuring learn how to properly stealth in their gear, or might gain training in persuasion after being hired by royalty and picking up on the word play and social cues.
